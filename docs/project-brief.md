# Project Brief

## Aim

**To investigate whether programmable mechanical stimulation applied to the body can reproducibly influence nociceptive processing and pain perception.**

## Context

Pain is continuously shaped by peripheral sensory input, attention, central processing, and endogenous pain-modulation mechanisms. Peripheral electrical neuromodulation and vibrotactile analgesia provide evidence that external sensory inputs can alter pain, but the role of the **spatial and temporal design of mechanical touch** remains comparatively underexplored.

The opportunity is to treat touch as an experimental neuromodulatory input rather than only as a communication channel.

## Scope

The initial study should stay deliberately small and mechanistic:

- Healthy participants
- Standardized experimental pain (e.g. pressure or thermal)
- Programmable mechanical stimulation
- Randomized within-subject comparisons
- A small number of well-controlled haptic conditions
- Sham / control conditions designed to separate sensory effects from distraction
- Repeat measurements to quantify individual and session variability

Likely primary outcomes:

- Pain intensity
- Pain unpleasantness
- Pain threshold
- After-effect once stimulation stops
- Test-retest repeatability

Optional exploratory measures:

- PPG / HRV
- EDA
- Skin temperature
- EEG or reflex-based measures if existing infrastructure makes this practical

## Team contribution

### Haptics
Defines the tactile parameter space and psychophysical design.

### Neuroscience
Defines the pain paradigm, relevant mechanisms, measurements, and interpretation.

### Mechatronics
Builds and characterizes a reproducible stimulation platform and data-acquisition system.

## What is explicitly out of scope for the first study

- Clinical migraine treatment
- Autonomous diagnosis
- Migraine forecasting
- A finished medical device
- Complex AI personalization
- Demonstration of chronic pain treatment
- Claims of nerve regeneration or preservation

These remain possible downstream research directions.

## Minimum useful result

The first study succeeds if it can determine whether **haptic pattern matters** and whether any observed effect is reproducible and distinguishable from simple distraction.

A null result is also informative if it constrains which mechanisms or stimulus strategies are worth pursuing.
