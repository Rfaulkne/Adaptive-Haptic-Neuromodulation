# Literature Map

This is a curated working map rather than a systematic review.

## A. Mechanical / haptic modulation of pain

### Staud et al. (2011)
**Attenuation of experimental pain by vibro-tactile stimulation in patients with chronic localized or widespread pain.**  
Investigated homotopic and heterotopic vibration during experimental pain. Important precedent for the possibility that tactile stimulation may influence pain beyond simple distraction.

PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21339076/

### Hollins, McDermott & Harper (2014)
**How does vibration reduce pain?**  
Examined whether vibration analgesia can be explained by distraction and explored spatial dependence of the effect.

PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24689133/

### Casale & Hansson (2022)
**The analgesic effect of localized vibration: a systematic review. Part 1: the neurophysiological basis.**  
Useful overview of proposed mechanisms, including Aβ-mediated sensory interactions.

PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35102735/

## B. Remote peripheral neuromodulation

### Yarnitsky et al. (2019)
**Remote Electrical Neuromodulation Relieves Acute Migraine: A Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled, Multicenter Trial.**  
Strong clinical proof-of-principle that peripheral stimulation at the upper arm can influence migraine pain remotely.

PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31074005/

### Marmura et al. (2023)
Remote electrical neuromodulation investigated as migraine prevention.

PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36704988/

## C. Pain sensing and physiological estimation

### Winslow et al. (2022)
**Automatic detection of pain using machine learning.**  
Demonstrates that physiological features can support machine classification of experimentally induced pain.

PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36438448/

### PainMonit dataset (2024)
Multimodal physiological dataset including BVP, EDA, skin temperature, ECG, EMG and respiration during experimental pain.

PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39333541/

## D. Migraine forecasting

### Wearable next-day migraine prediction (2025)
Empatica-derived physiology used in personalized prediction models; useful evidence that group-level biomarkers may be weak while individualized models can show signal.

PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41377954/

### HRV as a predictor of migraine (2026)
Sleep-time wearable HRV analysis of pre-migraine nights.

PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41607086/

### Forecasting migraine with time-series machine learning (2026)
Longitudinal mobile-health data used for headache forecasting.

PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41913100/

## E. Closed-loop pain / neuromodulation precedent

### Mekhail et al. (2020)
**Long-term safety and efficacy of closed-loop spinal cord stimulation to treat chronic back and leg pain (EVOKE).**  
Important precedent for feedback-controlled pain neuromodulation.

PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31870766/

### Shirvalkar et al. (2023)
**First-in-human prediction of chronic pain state using intracranial neural biomarkers.**  
Shows that individualized neural signals can be used to decode chronic pain state.

PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37217725/

## Working literature gap

The individual building blocks exist:

- Wearable physiological sensing
- Machine estimation of pain-related states
- Mechanical stimulation influencing pain
- Remote electrical neuromodulation
- Closed-loop neuromodulation

The less developed space is the combination of:

**programmable remote mechanical haptics + quantified nociceptive effects + individual response characterization**, with sensing and adaptive timing as later extensions.

This gap should be treated as a hypothesis to investigate rather than a claim that no prior work exists.
