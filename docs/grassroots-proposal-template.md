## 2. Project Topic, Objectives, Feasibility, and Potential Impact

### Aim

**To investigate whether programmable mechanical stimulation applied to the body can reproducibly influence nociceptive processing and pain perception.**

### Topic and Context

Pain is shaped not only by tissue damage or peripheral nerve activity, but also by sensory input, attention, central processing, and endogenous pain-modulation mechanisms.

Existing work in vibration analgesia, conditioned pain modulation, and remote electrical neuromodulation suggests that peripheral sensory stimulation can influence pain processing [1–4]. However, comparatively little is known about whether the **spatial and temporal design of mechanical touch itself** — including location, intensity, rhythm, movement, and predictability — can be deliberately engineered to alter nociceptive processing.

This project treats haptic stimulation not only as a communication modality, but as a controlled experimental input for studying interaction with the nervous system.

### Objectives

The initial project will investigate:

1. Whether remote mechanical haptic stimulation can measurably alter experimentally induced pain.
2. Whether different haptic patterns produce different effects.
3. Which stimulus parameters are most relevant, including frequency, amplitude, rhythm, spatial motion, location, and predictability.
4. Whether observed effects can be distinguished from simple distraction or attentional competition.
5. How much variability exists between individuals and repeated sessions.
6. Whether individual response profiles are sufficiently stable to justify later personalization.

### Feasibility

The project is intentionally scoped as an early-stage mechanistic study rather than a clinical treatment trial.

The initial study will use:

- Healthy participants
- Standardized experimental pain, such as pressure or thermal stimulation
- A programmable haptic prototype
- Randomized within-subject comparisons
- A small number of controlled haptic conditions
- Sham and/or attention-matched controls
- Repeat measurements where practical

Primary outcomes may include:

- Pain intensity
- Pain unpleasantness
- Pain threshold
- Effects during and after stimulation
- Tolerability
- Test-retest repeatability

Optional exploratory measures may include PPG/HRV, EDA, skin temperature, EEG, or reflex-based measures where existing resources make these practical.

### Potential Impact

The immediate outcome will be a controlled dataset describing whether and how engineered mechanical stimulation interacts with nociceptive processing.

If a reproducible effect is found, the work could provide a foundation for:

- Personalized haptic stimulation
- Non-invasive wearable neuromodulation
- Closed-loop sensing and intervention
- Migraine and recurrent pain applications
- Chronic primary or nociplastic pain research
- Sensory augmentation and rehabilitation
- Peripheral neuropathy and sensory-substitution systems

A negative or null result would also be valuable by constraining which mechanisms and stimulation strategies are worth pursuing.

## 3. Relevance to the Grassroots Program

The project is a natural fit for a Grassroots-style interdisciplinary program because the central research question exists **between disciplines**.

### Collaborative and Synergistic Nature

**Haptics** contributes the ability to design touch as a multidimensional experimental variable rather than a simple ON/OFF stimulus.

**Neuroscience** contributes the framework for understanding nociception, endogenous pain modulation, experimental pain paradigms, and interpretation of sensory responses.

**Mechatronics / Design & Integration** connects the intervention to the person using it. This role addresses how stimulation is embodied, worn, controlled, understood, tolerated, and ultimately adopted, while integrating the necessary sensors, actuators, electronics, and physical interfaces.

The collaboration creates a shared loop:

```text
Haptic design
      ↓
Controlled physical stimulation
      ↓
Neural + perceptual response
      ↓
User experience + system integration
      ↓
Measurement + interpretation
      ↓
Improved intervention design
```

The project therefore does more than combine three skill sets in parallel; each discipline directly changes how the others formulate and evaluate the research question.

## 4. Project Tasks and Responsibilities

### WP1 — Experimental Framing and Protocol
**Lead:** Neuroscience  
**Support:** Haptics, Design & Integration

- Define experimental pain paradigm
- Define primary and secondary outcome measures
- Select control and sham conditions
- Define inclusion/exclusion criteria
- Prepare ethics documentation if required

**Output:** Final study protocol

### WP2 — Haptic Stimulus Design
**Lead:** Haptics  
**Support:** Design & Integration

- Select initial haptic conditions
- Define stimulus parameters
- Develop spatial/temporal stimulation patterns
- Match perceived intensity where appropriate
- Establish a limited and interpretable experimental design space

**Output:** Validated haptic stimulus set

### WP3 — Wearable Prototype and System Integration
**Lead:** Mechatronics / Design & Integration  
**Support:** Haptics

- Develop wearable physical form
- Integrate actuators, electronics, controls, and data logging
- Characterize repeatability of stimulation
- Address comfort, placement, accessibility, and usability
- Develop a simple operator / participant interaction flow

**Output:** Experimental wearable platform

### WP4 — Pilot Testing
**Lead:** Entire team

- Small pilot with healthy participants
- Validate protocol timing and tolerability
- Identify confounds
- Refine haptic conditions and user interaction
- Confirm data acquisition workflow

**Output:** Finalized protocol and hardware iteration

### WP5 — Main Healthy-Participant Study
**Lead:** Neuroscience  
**Support:** Haptics, Design & Integration

Possible comparison conditions:

1. No stimulation
2. Continuous vibration
3. Spatially moving haptic pattern
4. Irregular / unpredictable pattern
5. Sham or attention-matched control

**Output:** Experimental dataset

### WP6 — Analysis and Interpretation
**Lead:** Neuroscience  
**Support:** Haptics

- Compare effects between stimulation conditions
- Quantify individual and session variability
- Assess persistence after stimulation stops
- Explore responder / non-responder patterns
- Evaluate whether haptic pattern has a reproducible effect

**Output:** Statistical analysis and research conclusions

### WP7 — Translation and Future-System Concept
**Lead:** Design & Integration  
**Support:** Entire team

- Translate findings into design requirements
- Identify usability and adoption barriers
- Define requirements for a future wearable
- Determine whether personalization or closed-loop sensing is justified
- Map next-stage applications

**Output:** Follow-up research and technology roadmap
