# Future Directions

These directions are intentionally downstream of the first mechanistic study.

## 1. Personalization

If a reproducible haptic analgesic effect exists:

- Identify responder / non-responder patterns
- Measure repeatability across sessions
- Determine whether baseline sensory thresholds or pain-modulation measures predict response
- Test whether individuals have stable preferred stimulus parameters

## 2. Closed-loop sensing

Long-term architecture:

```text
continuous sensing
        ↓
individual state estimate
        ↓
intervention timing
        ↓
optimized haptic stimulus
        ↓
measure response
        ↓
adapt
```

Candidate signals may include PPG / HRV, EDA, skin temperature, sleep, respiration, activity, or condition-specific signals.

The central challenge is not sensing alone but identifying a **specific enough control signal** to justify intervention.

## 3. Migraine

Potential future question:

**Can a pre-ictal or prodromal state be identified early enough to deliver a haptic intervention before significant pain develops?**

This requires both a proven intervention and a sufficiently reliable state-estimation method.

## 4. Endometriosis-associated pain

Potential direction:

**Can externally applied sensory stimulation modulate chronic or recurrent pain processing associated with endometriosis?**

This would address pain processing rather than the underlying disease pathology.

## 5. Diabetic peripheral neuropathy

A complementary branch of the platform may focus on **diminished** rather than excessive sensation.

Possible directions:

- Longitudinal sensory-threshold mapping
- Stochastic-resonance amplification of weak plantar sensation
- Sensory substitution from the foot to an intact body region
- Pressure / temperature / shear monitoring in areas with poor protective sensation
- Adaptive sensory training

This reframes the umbrella question:

> Can adaptive haptic interfaces interact with impaired sensory processing both when the nervous system produces too much sensation and when it produces too little?

## 6. Sensory augmentation / rehabilitation

The same experimental platform could support studies in:

- Body perception
- Sensory substitution
- Motor rehabilitation
- Peripheral sensory loss
- Human-machine sensory augmentation
